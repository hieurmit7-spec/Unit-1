Introduction

What is Active Reading?

Understanding the difference between truly reading and just looking at words

Why Active Reading Matters

Active Reading is a method of engaging with a text by questioning, annotating, summarizing, and connecting ideas as you read — rather than just letting your eyes move across the page.

Unlike passive reading (where you read without thinking deeply), active reading helps you understand, remember, and think critically about what you've read.

Through this unit, I discovered that reading is not a one-way street. It is a conversation between you and the author.

Core Skills

Skimming & Scanning

Two essential techniques every active reader must master

📚

Skimming

Reading quickly to get the gist (main idea) of a text. You look at headings, first sentences of paragraphs, and keywords without reading every word.

Example: Before an exam, you skim through all chapter titles to know which topics to review first.
🔍

Scanning

Reading to find specific details — like a name, date, or number. Your eyes move quickly across the page, searching for one piece of information.

Example: You scan a restaurant menu to find the price of your favorite dish.
Case Studies

What We Read

Three fascinating topics explored through active reading

Strategy

The Game of Go

Lateral Thinking & Strategy

An ancient board game that requires deep thinking. Unlike chess, Go requires players to think about the whole board, encouraging creative and flexible problem-solving.

Key Lesson It teaches "lateral thinking" — thinking creatively outside the rules. Players must build a smart strategy to control empty space, sometimes accepting a tactical loss to achieve eventual victory.
Psychology

Memories

Specific vs. Generic Memories

How our brain stores information in two main types. Generic memories are things we do every day. Episodic memories are special, one-time events.

Key Lesson Our memories are fluid — they can change over time. Active reading helps our brain create strong, specific memories. Life-threatening events are recorded deeply by the brain.
Society

Sports

How Games Unify a Nation

Games that can unite a whole nation. Sports are the "moral equivalent of war." They require courage and teamwork, offering a safe way to show national power.

Key Lesson Sports help build self-identity and unify different groups across many spheres of society — from politics to culture — creating a shared sense of pride.
